What Happened?

During the first months of 2017, sales recorded exceptionally high month-over-month growth rates, reaching over 100% at the beginning of the year. However, these growth rates gradually declined over the following months and eventually turned slightly negative in April (-3.9%).

At first glance, this pattern may suggest that business performance weakened after a strong start. However, total sales continued to trend upward overall, with only minor month-to-month fluctuations.

Why Did It Happen?

The sales decline observed in April was primarily driven by lower purchasing activity rather than lower spending per order. While Total Sales decreased by 3.9% from 368,341 to 353,843, Average Order Value increased from 140.7 to 148.9, indicating that customers who completed purchases spent more on average.

The decline was mainly volume-driven. Total Orders fell by 9.2% from 2,617 to 2,377, while Total Sold Units decreased by 10.6% from 2,975 to 2,660. This suggests that the business sold fewer products through fewer transactions during April.

A closer look at customer behavior reveals that Total Customers declined by 9.0% from 2,577 to 2,345. In addition, Purchase Frequency decreased from 1.15 to 1.13 orders per customer, meaning customers purchased slightly less often than in the previous month. Together, these changes explain most of the reduction in orders and units sold.

Several indicators may help explain the decline in customer activity. Median Item Price increased from 72.0 to 79.0 (+9.7%), potentially reducing purchase willingness among price-sensitive customers. At the same time, Average Delivery Days increased from 12.4 to 14.3 days (+15.3%), while Average Review Score 
decreased from 4.13 to 4.07, suggesting a slight deterioration in customer experience. These factors may have contributed to the reduction in customer 
participation during April.


Additional Observation

April's customer base remained heavily acquisition-driven, with 2,325 out of 2,345 customers (99.15%) being first-time buyers. This pattern is consistent with the broader behavior observed across the dataset and suggests that monthly performance is highly dependent on attracting new customers rather than repeat purchases

Summary

The apparent slowdown in early 2017 was largely a normalization effect rather than a deterioration in business performance. The exceptionally high growth rates at the beginning of the year were amplified by a weak sales base in late 2016, making subsequent growth appear weaker by comparison.

Although sales declined by 3.9% in April, the decline was primarily driven by lower customer activity, fewer orders, and fewer units sold, while spending per order continued to increase. Overall, the data suggests a temporary fluctuation within a broader growth trend rather than the start of a sustained downturn.

What Did I Learn?

This analysis reinforced the importance of looking beyond headline growth rates. Initially, the sharp growth at the beginning of 2017 appeared to indicate exceptional business performance. However, a deeper investigation revealed that much of this growth was driven by a weak comparison base in late 2016.

I also learned that a decline in growth rates does not necessarily mean a decline in business performance. While growth percentages gradually decreased and sales fell by 3.9% in April, the underlying trend remained positive, highlighting the importance of analyzing absolute performance alongside growth metrics.

Finally, identifying the drivers behind a sales decline requires breaking the problem into smaller components. By tracing sales performance through customers, orders, units sold, pricing, and delivery metrics, it became possible to distinguish between confirmed causes, contributing factors, and hypotheses that require further investigation.
