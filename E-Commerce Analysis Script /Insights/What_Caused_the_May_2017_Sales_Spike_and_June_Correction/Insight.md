`What Happened?`

During May 2017, total sales surged to $503,159, representing a 42.2% increase compared to April and a 36.6% increase compared to the March baseline ($368,341). This growth was accompanied by a substantial increase in customer activity, with total customers reaching 3,569 (+38.5% vs. March) and total orders rising to 3,640 (+39.1% vs. March). Sold units also increased significantly, indicating a sharp expansion in overall purchasing activity.

However, the strong momentum observed in May was followed by a correction in June. Total sales declined by 14.6% MoM, falling to $429,917, while total orders decreased by 12.0% to 3,205 orders. Sold units also dropped by 13.0%, reaching 3,571 units.

Despite this decline, June's performance remained well above the levels recorded before the May surge. Total sales in June were still 16.7% higher than March, suggesting that the June decline represented a partial correction of May's exceptional growth rather than a return to previous performance levels.

`Why Did It Happen?`

To investigate the drivers behind May’s performance spike and June’s subsequent correction, we separated our analysis into **Data-Backed Observations** (verified directly through internal business metrics) and **Evaluated Hypotheses** (deduced by testing and eliminating internal variables).

The data clearly identifies the core quantitative factors behind May's growth and June's pull-back:

Acquisition-Driven Volume Expansion:
The May revenue surge to **$503,159 (+36.6% vs. March baseline)** was driven entirely by order volume (**3,640 orders, +39.1%**) and sold units (**4,106 units, +38.0%**), while `Average Order Value (AOV)` slightly dropped from **$140.7 to $138.2**.

Driven by New Customers, Not Retention:
Growth came almost exclusively from first-time buyers. Total customers reached **3,569 (+38.5% vs. March)**, with **99.22% (3,541)** being new acquisitions. `Purchase Frequency` remained completely flat at **1.15**, confirming zero change in existing customer behavior

Pricing Was Not the Primary Driver:
Growth was not triggered by price cuts. The `Median Item Price` in May actually rose to **$74.9** (vs. **$72.0** in March) during the demand peak. Conversely, in June, despite `Median Item Price` dropping to **$69.9**, overall sales still contracted by **-14.6% MoM**. Lowering prices failed to stimulate demand, proving that pricing adjustments were not the catalyst.

Symmetrical Category Reversal:
Growth in May was concentrated in specific categories—led by `Computers Accessories` (+12.85% contribution to unit growth) and `Cool Stuff` (+11.30%). Crucially, these exact categories led the contraction in June (`Telephony` -12.20%, `Computers Accessories` -10.60%, and `Cool Stuff` -9.00%), demonstrating a symmetrical category-specific rise and fall.

Evaluated Hypotheses & Scenarios

Since internal metrics (price, delivery speed, and payment methods) do not reveal a single operational cause, we evaluated potential external and operational scenarios:

* **Hypothesis 1: Post-Purchase Operational Improvements (Eliminated as a Cause)**
  * *Assumption:* Improved delivery speeds or higher customer reviews stimulated initial sales.
  * *Evaluation:* Although `Average Delivery Days` improved (**10.9 days in May vs. 12.4 in March**) and `Average Review Score` slightly increased (**4.18 vs. 4.13**), these are post-purchase events. They reflect operational performance after order fulfillment and could not drive top-of-funnel conversion.

* **Hypothesis 2: Category-Targeted Customer Acquisition Campaign (Most Plausible Explanation)**
  * *Evaluation:* Given the absence of traffic, session, ad spend, and marketing attribution data, the exact external trigger cannot be definitively confirmed. However, the combination of:
    1. **99.22% new customer acquisition**
    2. **Stable purchase frequency**
    3. **Non-price-driven growth**
    4. **Concentrated category expansion followed by a symmetrical contraction**

  makes a temporary, category-targeted acquisition campaign the most plausible explanation for the observed pattern.
